# Measurement gate (G1) — decision record

**Date:** 2026-07-24
**Commit:** `f901bb5` (A1 live on all three edges)
**Decision:** **DEFER Part B (QUIC).** A2 **measured, not reproduced** after A1 —
including under a mobile-like 500 ms / 1% / 20 Mbit profile. No QUIC changes.

Upgraded from an earlier judgment-based deferral: this is now backed by a
measurement on a real beamd edge (raw data in `g1-local-2026-07-24/`).

## What was measured

A controlled reproduction on **real beamd processes**: a real `beamd serve` edge
container + a real shaped agent container on a docker bridge, netem applied to
the agent→edge leg **before** the agent dials, perfclient driving downloads from
the unshaped host. Harness: `scripts/perf-g1-local.sh` + `perf-g1-agent.sh`;
analyzer `test/perf/analyze.py` (fails closed; clean-control guard). 18 cases,
zero errors, zero corruption.

The A2 question: does a lone transfer under loss get penalized versus parallel
streams (which would keep the shared TCP connection ACK-clocked)?

| Profile | Case | Solo | 8-stream aggregate | solo/agg |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| clean (control) | 8 MiB | 10.29 MB/s | 11.67 MB/s | 88% |
| lossy 150 ms/1% | 8 MiB | 0.29 MB/s | 0.30 MB/s | **97%** |
| high-RTT+loss 500 ms/1%/20 Mbit | 512 KiB | 0.10 MB/s | 0.09 MB/s | **111%** |

- Under loss the pipe is loss-limited (~0.3 MB/s at 150 ms, ~0.1 MB/s at
  500 ms), and **a solo transfer gets essentially the whole pipe** — parallel
  streams do not beat it. That is the A2 test coming back negative.
- Small responses show **no 1/2/4 s timeout ladder** (36 B solo p95: 166 ms
  lossy, 474 ms high-RTT).
- The clean control is valid (solo 88% of parallel — no false positive), so the
  rig is not manufacturing artifacts. The earlier "solo = 59% of aggregate" was
  a shape-after-establishment bug, now fixed.

**Conclusion:** A1 (the 4 MiB window) was the real fix. A2 does not materially
bite beamd's transport even under adverse conditions. QUIC is not justified by
the measurement. Per §16 G1.6, **B1–B4 not currently justified.**

A2 is a **per-tunnel** property (each agent has its own TCP connection), so this
result holds regardless of user count. Many concurrent *users* is an edge
*capacity* question (Part B capacity limits), which is separate from A2/QUIC.

## Honest limitations (why this is strong but not the last word)

- **Controlled reproduction, not the production WAN link.** The transport
  mechanism is faithful (real beamd code), but a real WAN can differ.
- **Random loss, not bursty.** netem `loss 1%` is uniform; real networks
  sometimes have correlated/bursty loss, which punishes single-flow recovery
  more. A bursty-loss variant (`netem loss 1% 25%`) would stress A2 harder.
- **One faint sub-threshold hint:** at 253 KiB / high-RTT, the 8-stream
  aggregate (0.19 MB/s) beat solo (0.13) — solo/agg ≈ 68%. It is just under both
  the size cutoff (256 KiB) and the 70% threshold, and it **vanishes** at 512 KiB
  and 8 MiB, so it reflects a small transfer not fully filling a high-RTT pipe,
  not the pathological A2 that QUIC targets.

## Reopen / confirm criteria

The committed harness is ready. Escalate to a measured re-decision if:

- you want maximum confidence → run `scripts/perf-g1.sh` against a real *remote*
  edge (fresh droplet or a bounded prod run), and/or add a bursty-loss profile;
- real users begin reporting solo transfers stalling on lossy/high-RTT networks;
- concurrency/capacity at the edge (many tunnels) becomes the pain — note that
  is Part B capacity work, not A2.
